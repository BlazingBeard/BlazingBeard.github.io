The Problem

A cruise passenger has 6–8 hours in Ketchikan and $180 of souvenir budget. They spend it on a t-shirt made in Vietnam and a magnet they will not remember buying. Nothing they carry home proves they were here.

The Product

A foil trading card for something they actually did today. Rode the tram. Saw an eagle. Got soaked and laughed. They fill in the back — date, witness, story — and scan the QR to mint it to a digital shelf that follows them home.

The Multiplier

Every scan is an email address, a photo, a shareable card, and a person who now has an incomplete set. They come back to Alaska. They buy the Iceland deck. They tell four friends on the ship tonight at dinner.

3. Making it epic — visual spec

Print

  • Cold-foil holographic base under CMYK — rainbow only where the art breathes: water, aurora, the crest.
  • Raised spot-UV on the gold frame and the points medallion. Fingers must feel the rarity.
  • 350gsm linen stock; gold gilt edges on Legendary, black gilt on Mythic.
  • Letterpress fill-in back so a ballpoint bites the surface. Handwriting is the moat.
  • Micro-printed serial + QR. Every single card is uniquely numbered.

Art direction

  • WPA park poster × 1890s nautical chart × modern TCG frame.
  • Palette: deep spruce, storm slate, oxidized brass, parchment.
  • Every scene is weather-first. The weather is the antagonist.
  • Mythics go black core with conic prism foil — oil on wet asphalt.
  • Visitor Series uses a teal crest so locals can tell at a glance. That distinction is a feature.

Digital

  • Gyro-tilt holo viewer — the card on their phone reacts to their hand.
  • Pack-rip animation with haptics. The dopamine is the product.
  • AR: place your Mythic on the dinner table, three feet tall, aurora moving.
  • Auto-generated share card sized for Instagram Stories, with the ship's name on it.
  • Ship leaderboards: "Ovation of the Seas — 1,284 cards claimed today."
